01 / The idea

Connection is at the heart of everything.

An interactive installation exploring the relationship between people, nature and technology. Two sculptural trees reach toward one another, their branches combining to create a heart.

01 — PEOPLE

Made by you

The work responds to visitors rather than asking them to simply observe. Each heartbeat becomes part of the artwork.

02 — NATURE

Organic forms

Tree-like shapes and open wire mesh evoke living branches, while concealing a structural truss framework.

03 — TECHNOLOGY

Visible emotion

Heartbeat sensors, responsive LEDs and an evolving soundscape translate personal rhythms into a shared experience.

02 / Visitor journey

From stillness to togetherness.

The presentation describes five stages of interaction, beginning in a quiet resting state and ending with the sculpture gently returning to calm.

STAGE 01

A moment of calm

Both trees wait in a soft, inviting glow.

STAGE 02

The first spark

One visitor touches a sensor; their tree awakens and pulses.

STAGE 03

Two rhythms

A second visitor joins. Light moves up both trees toward the heart.

STAGE 04

Coming together

The central heart illuminates, joining two individual interactions.

STAGE 05

Back to stillness

As hands leave the sensors, the light and sound slowly fade.

03 / Form & materials

Engineered to feel alive.

A truss-supported structure is shaped into two trees, with chicken wire creating flexible, natural-looking forms. LED strips trace the branches, while internal uplighting reveals depth and texture.

The team's presentation proposes a structure approximately 8–9 ft tall, with a 6 ft arch width. Material experiments included chicken wire, reflective mesh, iridescent organza, and different approaches to illuminating mesh.

Visual direction

Gentle, not overwhelming.

Soft transitions and thoughtful colour help create an intimate, garden-like atmosphere. The team deliberately wants to avoid harsh strobes, visible technical clutter, abrupt switching and passive, look-only art.

04 / Behind the experience

Technology in service of feeling.

The proposed system translates live heartbeat readings into lighting and audio, with the technical elements kept discreet so visitors can focus on the sculpture and one another.

SENSING

Heartbeat input

The team's plan explores Arduino-based pulse sensing, with an electrode-based hand-contact approach also under development and pulse sensors as a backup.

CONTROL

Data to light

Arduino feeds heartbeat data into Chataigne; Resolume handles pixel mapping, with Art-Net sending output to LED controllers and lighting.

SOUND

An evolving soundscape

Ableton Live is planned to turn pulse information into sound, combining heartbeat textures, breathing, nature and soft musical tones.

REMOTE PARTICIPATION

Twitch integration

Planned Twitch chat commands allow remote viewers to trigger selected lighting effects when the sculpture is dormant.

OUTDOOR PRODUCTION

Weather-conscious design

Weatherproof enclosures, appropriately rated cabling and outdoor speaker protection form part of the technical planning.

PROJECT RESEARCH

Anonymous interaction counts

The presentation proposes recording participation totals and average heart-rate data without collecting names, for future research by Waves.

05 / Proposed setting

Blue Peter Garden,
MediaCity, Salford.

The team selected a sheltered-feeling space toward the back of the garden, near the tramline, surrounded by trees, bushes and raised planters. The natural surroundings are intended to frame the illuminated heart.
